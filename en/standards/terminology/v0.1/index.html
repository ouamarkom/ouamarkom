Conceptual Specifications

4. The Command Economy of AI™ (CE-AI)

Official Definition: The Command Economy of AI™ is an emerging economic and architectural framework for exploring how economic activity is structured when artificial intelligence becomes capable of acting directly on behalf of institutions, focusing on the Economic Command as the primary unit linking intent, authority, execution, evidence, and outcomes.

Methodological Rule: In Specification v0.1, CE-AI is treated as a research hypothesis rather than an established commercial fact.

Permitted Formulations:

  • «CE-AI is an emerging framework and research hypothesis.»
  • «CE-AI is a proposed architectural framework for AI execution.»

Prohibited Formulations: Do not assert «CE-AI is the established new global economy» prior to conclusive independent empirical evidence.

5. Economic Command

Official Definition: An Economic Command is a governed unit of delegated institutional intent that binds an economic objective to authority, constraints, execution, evidence, and an expected verifiable outcome.

Core Distinction: An Economic Command is not a Prompt, Task, Agent, Workflow, or API call. It represents a higher-level governed unit linking:

Intent ➔ Authority ➔ Execution ➔ Evidence ➔ Outcome

6. AI Agent

Official Definition: An AI Agent is an execution actor capable of making local decisions and executing tasks using tools and data within defined bounds.

Within CE-AI: Agent = Execution Capability. An agent is not treated as the primary economic accounting unit.

Structural Relationship: Command ➔ coordinates ➔ Agents. A single Economic Command may orchestrate multiple agents.

8. Prompt vs. Command

A Prompt is a natural language text input provided to a model. An Economic Command is a structured, governed system entity containing:

  • Economic objective & institutional context.
  • Delegated economic authority & spending limits.
  • Operational scope, constraints, and risk classification.
  • Time-to-live and approval requirements.
  • Required evidence structure and verification criteria.
Prompt ≠ Command

9. Delegation vs. Authorization

Delegation (التفويض): Assigns overall responsibility and operational mandate to act for an objective.

Authorization (التخويل): Explicit technical permission allowing a specific action to take place.

«Delegation establishes responsibility. Authorization permits action.»

10 & 11. Delegated Authority & Authority Envelope

Delegated Economic Authority (DEA): The precise operational bounds granted by an enterprise to an AI system (e.g., maximum budget limits, preferred vendors, negotiation boundaries, risk thresholds).

Authority Envelope: The dynamic boundary defining: "What the AI may do, where, when, under which conditions, and up to what economic risk."

12 & 13. Agency vs. Autonomy Separation

Governed Economic Agency: The system's capacity to direct multi-step economic activity on behalf of the institution.

Autonomy: The degree of freedom granted to act without human intervention.

«Agency ≠ Autonomy»

High Agency + Low Autonomy Example: An AI system managing a SAR 1,000,000 procurement savings strategy (High Agency), but requiring explicit human approval for every PO exceeding SAR 50,000 (Low Autonomy).

14. Earned Autonomy

Systems do not receive elevated operational autonomy merely because they possess higher LLM intelligence scores. Autonomy expands progressively based on observable performance, evidence, policy compliance, and verified outcomes.

«Capability enables action. Authority permits action. Evidence earns trust. Verification validates outcomes. Performance earns autonomy.»

Execution, Verification & Architecture

15. Economic Execution

The operational process of translating an economic objective into real transactional effects within business systems.

Not merely: "AI suggests a vendor list."

Rather: "AI evaluates vendors ➔ negotiates terms within DEA ➔ selects vendor ➔ generates purchase order ➔ routes approval ➔ executes transaction ➔ records evidence."

16 - 18. Evidence & Outcome Verification

Evidence: Audit-ready execution logs, cryptographic timestamps, API responses, human sign-offs, and state logs.

Outcome Verification: Independent evaluation confirming that the declared outcome meets preset success criteria.

  • Execution Verified: Confirms the sequence took place.
  • Outcome Verified: Confirms the target state was attained.
  • Outcome Attributed: Determines how much of the outcome was caused by the AI execution.
«Outcome Verified ≠ Outcome Caused by AI»

19 - 21. Smart Hand™ & Institutional Gateway

Institutional Gateway: The secure perimeter controlling identity, authorization, risk thresholds, and escalation pathways between the AI execution engine and enterprise core systems.

Smart Hand™ Positioning: Smart Hand™ is Ouamarkom's primary experimental system for testing CE-AI hypotheses in live environments.

«Smart Hand™ = Economic Execution Engine»

In Specification v0.1, Smart Hand™ is strictly positioned as an "Experimental system for testing governed AI-driven economic execution," not a general AI OS or generic agent marketplace.

Advanced Operational Specifications

24. Evidence vs. Verification Rule

«Evidence makes execution observable. Verification makes outcomes trustworthy.»

The terms shall not be used interchangeably (Evidence ≠ Verification).

25. Outcome Tri-State Classification

  • Observed Outcome: The system detected a change or metric delta.
  • Verified Outcome: The observed delta met predefined validation parameters.
  • Attributed Outcome: Causal evidence links the delta directly to the AI execution.

27. Authority Recalibration

The process of dynamically adjusting an Authority Envelope based on: Performance + Evidence + Risk + Verification.

Example: Upon successful completion of 100 low-risk purchase commands without error, an AI agent's authorization envelope may be recalibrated from SAR 10,000 to SAR 25,000 under defined policy criteria.
